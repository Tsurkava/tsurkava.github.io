How to buy property in Georgia as a foreigner (2026 guide)

Short answer: foreigners can buy apartments, houses and commercial property in Georgia freely, the deal is registered in one to four business days, and there is no transfer tax. Below are the rules and steps as of November 2026.

Who can buy

  • Foreign citizens have the same property rights as Georgian citizens.
  • Exception: agricultural land cannot be bought directly by foreigners. Some house plots are classified as agricultural, so the land category must be checked before signing.
  • No residence permit, visa or local partner is required to buy.

Step by step

  • Choose the property and check it: ownership extract from the Public Registry, encumbrances, building permit and developer track record for new builds.
  • Sign a preliminary agreement and pay a deposit (for new builds — according to the developer's installment plan).
  • Sign the purchase agreement at a Public Service Hall or before a notary.
  • Registration with the National Agency of Public Registry (NAPR): usually 1–4 business days depending on the chosen speed.
  • You receive an extract from the Public Registry — this is the proof of ownership.

Costs and taxes

  • No transfer tax or stamp duty on purchase.
  • Registration fee at the Public Registry (depends on registration speed).
  • Notary fees if a notary or a power of attorney is used.
  • Annual property tax depends on the owner's income and the property; rental income of individuals is usually taxed at a reduced rate once registered — check your case with a lawyer or accountant.

Residence permit through property

  • Since 1 March 2026 the minimum property value for a one-year renewable residence permit is USD 150,000 (previously USD 100,000). The value is confirmed by an accredited appraiser.
  • An investment of USD 300,000 or more can qualify for a 5-year investment residence permit.
  • Applications are submitted via the Public Service Hall.

Buying remotely

  • Viewings by video call.
  • Signing through a representative with a notarized (and, if needed, apostilled) power of attorney.
  • Payment by bank transfer according to the contract.
